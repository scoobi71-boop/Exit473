Spicemas — Grenada's Grand Celebration

Every August, the Isle of Spice transforms. The streets fill with the wail of calypso, the shimmer of steel pan, the thunder of masquerade bands, and the irresistible energy of a people celebrating who they are. This is Spicemas.

Calypso — The Voice of the People

Sharp wit. Unfiltered truth. Irresistible melody. Calypso is Grenada's living editorial page, sung aloud for all to hear.

History & Heritage

The Art of Speaking Truth in Song

Calypso arrived in Grenada through the shared waters of the Caribbean, carrying its roots from West African griot traditions and the plantation-era practice of singing what could not be spoken. What took root on this island grew into something distinctly Grenadian — wry, fearless, poetic, and unmistakably tied to the land and its people.

Every year during Spicemas, the calypso tent becomes the most important stage in Grenada. Artists compete through weeks of semi-finals and finals, culminating in the prestigious Calypso Monarch Competition on Dimanche Gras Sunday — the night before Carnival Monday. A calypso Monarch title is among the highest honors a Grenadian artist can receive.

The tradition is built on the two-verse structure: two composed verses that carry the narrative, and a repeated chorus the crowd knows by heart within minutes. In the space of four minutes, a master calypsonian can indict a corrupt minister, celebrate a national hero, grieve a community tragedy, or make ten thousand people laugh until they weep.

🏆
Competition
The Calypso Monarch competition is held on Dimanche Gras Sunday, the night before Carnival Monday — one of the most watched events of Spicemas.
🎤
The Calypso Tent
Weeks before the final, artists perform at calypso tents — live shows where audiences hear new compositions and pick their favourites.
📜
Social Commentary
Calypso remains the people's newspaper. Topics range from politics and injustice to love, humor, and celebration of island life.
🌍
Caribbean Roots
Grenada shares the calypso tradition with Trinidad & Tobago, Barbados, and other Eastern Caribbean nations — each island bringing its own flavor.
"Calypso is not entertainment — it is history, it is news, it is philosophy. When the calypsonian picks up the microphone, the whole island is listening."
— Grenadian calypso tradition

Steel Pan — Music Born from Ingenuity

Hammered from oil drums into one of the world's greatest instruments. Steel pan is the sound of Caribbean genius — joyful, precise, and alive.

History & Sound

From the Yard to the Stage

Steel pan was invented in Trinidad in the 1930s and 1940s by Afro-Caribbean communities who were forbidden from using traditional drums. Working with discarded oil drums, they discovered that hammering the surface created distinct musical pitches — and the instrument that would define Caribbean music was born.

Grenada embraced steel pan deeply. Pan orchestras — called steel bands — are a cornerstone of Carnival, performing during Panorama competitions and road march processions. The shimmer of a soprano pan playing a melody over the thunderous bass pans below is one of the most distinctive sounds in all of Grenada.

Steel bands compete in the annual Panorama competition, where each orchestra arranges a soca or calypso tune and performs it live before judges and thousands of fans. The arrangements can run ten minutes long and involve dozens of musicians playing with extraordinary precision and feeling.

🎼
Panorama
The Panorama competition puts steel bands head to head. Arrangements are judged on musicality, rhythm, and crowd energy — it's pure spectacle.
🔨
Pan Types
A full steel band includes soprano pans (melody), double tenors, guitar pans (harmony), and massive bass pans that shake the ground.
🏫
School Bands
Pan is taught in Grenadian schools, ensuring the next generation carries the tradition. School steel bands compete in their own category during Spicemas.
🌅
Jouvert Morning
Steel bands roll through the streets from 4am during Jouvert, their music echoing off the buildings as the sun rises over St. George's Harbour.
"You don't just hear steel pan — you feel it in your chest, in your feet, in the air around you. It is the heartbeat of Carnival."
— Grenada steel pan tradition
The Instruments

Inside the Steel Orchestra

🎵
Soprano Pan (Ping Pong)

The melody voice of the orchestra. Carries the main tune with bright, bell-like tones. The most recognizable steel pan sound.

Melody
🎶
Double Tenor Pan

Two pans played together for rich harmonic range. Often carries counter-melodies and fills in the middle register of the orchestra.

Harmony
🎸
Guitar & Cello Pans

The rhythm section of the pan family, providing chordal support and keeping the harmonic foundation underneath the melody.

Rhythm Harmony
💥
Bass Pan

Six or more large pans that produce the deep, powerful bass notes. When the bass pans hit, you feel it before you hear it.

Masquerade — Where Costume Becomes Identity

From the ancient devils of Jouvert to the dazzling feathers of fantasy bands — masquerade is how Grenada transforms itself during Carnival.

The Mas Tradition

Costume as Storytelling

In Grenada, "playing mas" — joining a masquerade band and taking to the streets in costume — is an act of cultural pride, creative expression, and communal joy. Each mas character type carries its own history, symbolism, and movement. From the ancestral spirits of the Jab Jab to the elaborate fantasies of modern bands, masquerade is Carnival's most visual art form.

Carnival Characters & Bands
😈
Jab Jab (Devil Mas)

The most iconic character of Grenada Carnival. Bodies covered in grease, oil, or paint — black, blue, or silver — with horns, chains, and pitch forks. The Jab Jab represents the spirit of rebellion and liberation.

🪞
Short Knee (Traditional Mas)

A traditional masquerade character in an elaborate costume adorned with mirrors, beads, and colorful fabrics cut at the knee. A beloved part of the old mas tradition that connects to European carnival heritage.

🪶
Wild Indian

Stunning feathered headdresses, intricate beadwork, and fierce painted faces. The Wild Indian mas is a powerful visual tradition that pays homage to the indigenous peoples of the Caribbean.

🔵
Blue Devil (Jab Molassi)

Covered in molasses or dark paint, the Blue Devil prowls the Jouvert streets in the early morning hours — a creature of the night that the first daylight of Carnival Monday chases away.

✨
Fantasy Bands

Modern carnival bands with elaborate themes, thousands of sequins, feathers, and jewels. Fantasy mas brings world-class costume design to the Grenada road march — vivid, dazzling, and spectacular.

👑
Old Mas Characters

Satirical characters that poke fun at society — the Midnight Robber with his boastful speeches, the Pierrot Grenade, the Dame Lorraine. Old Mas keeps carnival's tradition of social commentary alive in costume form.

"When you put on the mas, you become something larger than yourself. You carry the history of your people in your costume, and you dance it through the streets for the whole island to see."
— Grenada masquerade tradition

Traditions — The Rhythm of Spicemas

From Jouvert at 4am to the last road march chant — Spicemas is a living calendar of traditions that bind generations together.

The Spicemas Calendar

How Grenada Celebrates Carnival

Grenada Carnival — officially called Spicemas — takes place over two days in August: Carnival Monday and Carnival Tuesday. But the season begins weeks earlier with calypso tent shows, queen shows, and competitions that build anticipation across the island. Each tradition has its own energy, its own crowd, its own sacred meaning.

🌅
Jouvert (J'ouvert)

The explosive start to Carnival Monday. Beginning around 4–5am, revelers covered in oil, paint, and grease pour into the streets with Jab Jab characters, steel bands, and soca trucks. Jouvert is raw, ancient, and electric.

Monday Morning · 4am
🚶
Road March

The main daytime parade on Carnival Monday and Tuesday. Costumed bands march through the streets of St. George's to thumping soca music from flatbed trucks. The Road March song — the tune played most at the judging points — wins its own crown.

Monday & Tuesday
🌙
Dimanche Gras

The grand Sunday night show before Carnival Monday. Features the Calypso Monarch competition, the Soca Monarch competition, and the Carnival Queen pageant — three crowns in one spectacular evening.

Sunday Night
👸
Carnival Queen Pageant

Young women compete in elaborate carnival costumes on the Dimanche Gras stage, judged on beauty, stage presence, and the artistry of their mas costume — some of the most breathtaking gowns in the Caribbean.

Pageant
🏆
Soca Monarch

The soca version of the Calypso Monarch competition. Artists are judged on their soca composition and live performance energy. The Power category (up-tempo) and Groovy category (slower, melodic soca) each crown their own champion.

Competition
🍛
Carnival Street Food

Oil-down, roti, bakes and saltfish, corn soup, roasted corn, fried chicken — the streets of Grenada become a moving feast during Spicemas. Every corner has a vendor; every smell is irresistible.

Food & Culture
🔚
Last Lap

The final road march procession on Carnival Tuesday evening. As sunset approaches, the Last Lap brings Spicemas to an emotional close. The energy is bittersweet — a full year until the next one.

Tuesday Evening
🥁
Panorama

The premier steel pan competition of Spicemas. Steel bands — sometimes 100+ musicians strong — perform live arrangements judged on musicality, rhythm and crowd appeal. One of the Caribbean's great musical events.

Steel Pan Competition

— Grenada Spicemas tradition
